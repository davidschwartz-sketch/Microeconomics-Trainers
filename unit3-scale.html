Scale and the Long Run

In the long run the size of the plant is a choice, not a given. Which size is cheapest depends on how much you want to make — and the long-run average total cost curve is what you get by always choosing the best one.

Four ideas. Returns to scale LRATC = the lower envelope Economies and diseconomies of scale Minimum efficient scale
Scale every input together and output may more than double, exactly double, or less than double. Each possible plant has its own U-shaped short-run ATC; the long-run curve runs underneath all of them, touching each at the one output that plant is best for — not at that plant’s own lowest point. Its falling stretch is economies of scale, its rising stretch diseconomies, and minimum efficient scale is the smallest output at which it reaches its lowest value.
