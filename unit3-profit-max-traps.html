Finding the Profit-Maximizing Quantity

Eight price-taking firms, eight complete tables, one question each. The arithmetic is already done for you — the whole difficulty is knowing which row to stop at, and there are three well-worn ways to stop at the wrong one.

The rule. MR = P for a price-taker Make the unit if MC ≤ MR Stop at the LAST such unit
A small firm in a competitive market sells all it wants at the going price without moving it, so every extra unit adds exactly that price to revenue: MR = P. Make a unit whenever it brings in at least what it costs, and keep going while that is still true.
The three ways to get this wrong Chasing revenue to the bottom of the table, stopping at the fattest margin instead of the last positive one, and hunting for a row where MC exactly equals MR — which, with whole units, usually does not exist. Worth reading once you have finished a run; reading it first does half the work for you.
