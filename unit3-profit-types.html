Accounting Profit and Economic Profit

Two businesses can keep identical books and still face opposite correct decisions, because what they each gave up to be in business differs. Accounting profit cannot see that. Economic profit can — which is why the decision is made on economic profit.

The chain. Explicit costs: money paid out Implicit costs: uses given up Accounting profit = Revenue − Explicit Economic profit = Accounting − Implicit
An implicit cost is what a resource the owner already owns — their time, their land, their money — could have been earning elsewhere. No money changes hands, and it is a real cost all the same. Because implicit costs are never negative, economic profit is never above accounting profit. Economic profit above zero says stay, below zero says switch, and exactly zero is normal profit: the owner is earning precisely what their resources would earn in their next-best use.
